    trnfft: spherical harmonic transforms, and how close plain PyTorch gets on Trainium¶

    trnfft's new spherical harmonic transform, trnfft.sht, runs its forward 8.5–28× faster than fp64 ducc0 on the same Trn2 box, while its inverse is 26× slower than its own forward at L = 511, F = 1024, nearly all of the gap spent in two torch.flip calls. The Legendre stage is a ragged batch of matmuls against fixed tables, and the hand-written NKI kernel that holds those tables stationary in SBUF beats a carefully padded plain-PyTorch version by only 1.2–2.2× at large batch, and loses to it by 1.5–1.6× at one field: what plain PyTorch can't do on the device is the FFT, and the naive per-m loop never gets past compile. This is for anyone weighing Trainium for spectral-transform models in weather and climate, and for anyone deciding how much of that work needs a library at all.

    Status

    The SHT is trnfft#152, released in trnfft v0.28.0. Measurements: trn2.3xlarge, public Neuron SDK 2.33 (TorchNeuron, torch 2.14, NKI 0.7.0), one logical core, at the device-validated head 18905ae (results). This is milestone 1 of the spectral-transform exemplar, rank 3 on the flagship-exemplar list.

    The problem¶

    Spectral-transform atmosphere models run forward and inverse SHTs every time step. Each SHT is a real FFT along every latitude ring, then a transform along latitude for each zonal wavenumber m against a table of associated Legendre functions P_lm. At weather resolution (L ≈ 1000–1300) the latitude stage is O(L³) per field and dominates (trnsci#111).

    The CPU references, ducc0 and SHTns, are mature fp64 codes. On the neuron device, the textbook algorithm in PyTorch gets this:

    Route on the Trn2 device What happens Source
    torch.fft for the longitude stage Not usable on the neuron device. rfft ran, but irfft raised "complex dtypes are not supported natively on Neuron" with CPU fallback restricted; otherwise it falls back to the CPU. trnfft#157 results, trnfft#82
    Per-m aten loop for the latitude stage 2,046 matmul calls for a forward plus an inverse at L = 511, with 511 distinct matmul shapes and 769 distinct op signatures (1,023 / 1,537 at L = 1023). The cold run at L = 511, F = 1 exceeded a 19-minute slot. No warm number exists. trnfft#152 results, trnfft#157
    Padded aten latitude stage (one bmm per size class, 10 classes) 20 calls, 19 shapes, 35 op signatures at every L. Executes 1.10× (L = 511) and 1.21× (L = 1023) the kernel's flops. Cold first call 24–83 s forward. Same accuracy as the kernel. trnfft#157 results
    trnfft.sht, NKI latitude kernels One launch at L = 511, three at L = 1023. Numbers below. trnfft#152

    On a CPU the per-m loop is merely slow. On the device each new shape is a new compiled program, about 770 of them; padding into ten size classes cuts that to about 35. The padded row still uses trnfft's FFTs, because torch.fft doesn't run there.

    What the architecture suggests¶

    Three properties of the latitude stage line up with the NeuronCore.

    The table is the stationary operand. After the even/odd fold (P_lm(−x) = (−1)^(l+m) P_lm(x)), each m splits into two problems p = 2m + q, each a GEMM a_p = P_p · X_p of K_p ≈ (L − m + 1)/2 degrees by H ≈ L/2 northern latitudes. P_p is the same for every field, so it belongs in SBUF, loaded once, while all F fields stream through the Tensor Engine. Arithmetic intensity is about L flops per field element. With F ≥ 128 the stage is compute-bound, and the table crosses HBM once per transform whatever F is.

    No complex arithmetic reaches the device. The table is real, so the coefficients' real and imaginary parts are just 2F columns of the moving operand, and the missing complex dtype never comes up.

    The sparsity is static and tile-shaped. Near the poles, P_lm for large m underflows. Which 128 × 128 tiles are all zero depends only on L and the grid, so it can be a compile-time mask, with the tile as the unit of skipping: the same idea trnsparse uses for screened tiles.

    PSUM accumulates in fp32, so each output rounds once after its K-term sum. That is what puts the device error at the CPU's fp32 level rather than above it.

    The approach¶

    trnfft#152 builds the SHT from pieces trnfft already had, plus two new kernels:

    Piece Design
    Longitude trnfft.rfft / trnfft.irfft on trnfft's NKI FFTs
    Latitude sht_legendre_forward_kernel / sht_legendre_inverse_kernel in trnfft/nki/sht.py: a ragged grouped GEMM over all 2(L+1) problems with static shapes. trnblas's grouped GEMM is the general form
    Polar tiles entries below 1e-30 of the per-m maximum dropped; all-zero tiles neither loaded nor multiplied. Skips 3.9% of tiles (0.7% of flops) at L = 511, 11.3% (9.4%) at L = 1023
    Both physical cores at LNC=2 each program takes half of the 2F columns, so both run identical shapes and masks
    Tables fp64 three-term recurrence on the host, stored fp32. P_mm starts in log space as mantissa × 2^e, because sin^m θ at m ≈ 1000 underflows fp64 near the poles before the recurrence can grow P_lm back

    The deliberate tradeoff is generality for compile stability: shapes, chunking and mask are baked into a plan string, so each (L, grid) compiles once and any other L is a new compile. Chunking caps a launch at 8192 live tiles (three launches at L = 1023); one launch and three gave the same warm time (176.8 vs 177.9 ms forward).

    Implementation¶

    The core of the analysis kernel, from trnfft/nki/sht.py at 5cf7da5. tt holds the transposed, stationary table tiles for problem p; live is the static mask; the loop body runs once per column tile of up to 512 of the 2F columns:

    for kt in range(kt_n):
    ks = min(SHT_TILE, k - kt * SHT_TILE)
    hv = [h for h in range(ht_n) if live[kt][h]]
    sb = nl.ndarray((ks, ns), dtype=nl.float32, buffer=nl.sbuf)
    if hv:
        ps = nl.zeros((ks, ns), dtype=nl.float32, buffer=nl.psum)
        for h in hv:
            nisa.nc_matmul(
                dst=ps, stationary=tt[(kt, h)], moving=xs[h], accumulate=True
            )
        nisa.tensor_copy(dst=sb, src=ps)
    else:
        nisa.memset(dst=sb, value=0.0)
    row = o - base + q + 2 * kt * SHT_TILE
    nl.store(out[row : row + 2 * ks - 1 : 2, c0 + n0 : c0 + n0 + ns], value=sb)

    The mask check is Python at trace time, so a skipped tile emits no instructions, and the store writes one parity of degrees as a stride-2 DMA rather than a gather. Table tiles are transposed once per problem with nisa.nc_transpose, avoiding nl.load_transpose2d (aws-neuron-sdk#1452). The mask travels in plan, a string argument, because closure-captured values are not in the NKI trace-cache key (aws-neuron-sdk#1454). A poisoned-table simulator test (masked tiles hold NaN) shows skipped tiles are never read.

    What didn't work¶

    The inverse. At L = 511, F = 1024 the inverse takes 7.8 s against a 0.30 s forward. Its Legendre kernel takes 4.0 ms per 256-field chunk, the same as the forward's. The other 1.94 s per chunk is trnfft.irfft, and nearly all of that is the Hermitian fill that rebuilds the full spectrum before an inverse complex FFT (trnfft#156):

    full_re[..., half:] = torch.flip(re[..., 1 : 1 + num_neg], dims=[-1])
full_im[..., half:] = -torch.flip(im[..., 1 : 1 + num_neg], dims=[-1])

    One device torch.flip of a (256, 512, 511) fp32 tensor takes 1,053.7 ms; the full complex ifft takes 186.6 ms. Two lines of reversal cost about eleven times the transform they prepare. The unconfirmed guess is that a last-axis flip lowers to a gather. The fix path in #156 is to stop materialising the conjugate half, with a half-length c2r FFT (trnfft#124) or a reversed-index load inside an NKI kernel.

    The plain-PyTorch row, and the margin it left. The planned device-aten row, the per-m loop on the device, never produced a number: its cold run at L = 511, F = 1 exceeded the 19-minute slot. The padded baseline in trnfft#157, built as the fairer comparison, narrowed the case for the kernel. End to end at L = 511 the two rows are within 3%, because the inverse longitude stage costs both of them 2.0 s per 256-field chunk. The kernel's advantage is confined to the latitude stage, and it reverses at F = 1.

    Rows that didn't complete. L = 1023 at LNC=1 is unverified: its cold compile did not finish inside a 19-minute slot. L = 1023, F = 1024 didn't complete in either row. For the kernel, compile plus four slow inverse chunks exceeded the slot. For padded aten, the host OOM killer stopped neuronx-cc compiling the zero-pad (aten::constant_pad_nd on [256, 1024, 1024]) in the SHT's inverse longitude stage; at 64-field chunks it ran out of device memory, as the benchmark keeps about 13 GB resident (results). The NEFFs from the window are kept for a pre-warmed retry (trnsci#118).

    One reference, not two. SHTns didn't build on the box (it needs libfftw3-dev), so device accuracy rests on ducc0 alone. On the CPU the fp64 path agrees with both to 1e-12.

    Upstream asks for AWS Neuron. (1) Lower a last-axis torch.flip on the neuron device to a reversed-stride DMA, or document that it gathers: 1.05 s for 67M elements is a trap. (2) aws-neuron-sdk#1454: include closure-captured values in the trace-cache key. (3) A grouped or ragged matmul in aten would make the per-m loop one compiled program instead of hundreds, without the padded version's 10–21% extra flops.

    Fit. The latitude stage is a good fit: stationary real tables, fp32 accumulation, static tile sparsity, and enough fields to make it compute-bound. The surrounding stages fit less well: at L = 1023, F = 128 the forward's Legendre kernels take 20.4 ms of 177.9, against 58.4 ms of FFT and 151.9 ms of longitude fold, permute and weights. Small L is FFT- and data-movement-bound, as trnsci#111 predicted. Scale-out is ensembles, not resolution: cross-core transfers go through the host (aws-neuron-sdk#1451).

    Why the kernel wins at large F. On the same flop count, the latitude stage runs at 8.56 against 5.59 TFLOP/s at L = 511, F = 1024. Padding and polar tiles explain only 1.10× of that 1.53×. The rest is how the work runs: each table tile is loaded into SBUF once and stays stationary while every column tile accumulates its K-sum in fp32 PSUM, and degree rows go out as stride-2 DMA. The padded inverse also does an index_select per class; these runs don't isolate its cost.

    Why padded aten wins at F = 1. The kernel's latitude time at L = 1023 barely moves from F = 1 to F = 128 (19.7 to 20.4 ms), so at one field it is a fixed per-transform cost: three launches that load and transpose every live tile of a 1.07 GB table. Padded aten's fixed cost is lower (12.2 ms); the data doesn't separate launch overhead from table traffic. At L = 511, F = 1 the latitude stages are even, yet padded aten is faster end to end (6.33 vs 11.8 ms), a difference outside the stage the rows differ in, and unexplained.

    What's next¶

    • The inverse (trnfft#156), then a half-cost real FFT (trnfft#124). Once the flip is gone the inverse should land near the forward.
    • The kernel's fixed cost at small F, the open question from the padded-aten comparison (trnfft#157): whether to cut it or route small batches to the padded path.
    • L = 1023 at LNC=1 and the L = 1023, F = 1024 row, from a pre-warmed NEFF cache (trnsci#118).
    • The rest of milestone 1 (trnfft#148, trnsci#111): the vector SHT, then smooth longitude sizes such as 1280 and 2560 for L = 1279 (trnfft#149), and a shallow-water ensemble with trnrand counter streams.
    • trnfft phase trackers: Phase 2 — precision, Phase 3 — plan reuse, streaming, NEFF cache, Phase 4 — multi-chip, Phase 5 — trn2 larger-SBUF path.

    Suite context: trnsci.dev, the roadmap, the suite-wide tracker. Earlier posts: launch cost, batch fusion.

    Takeaway¶

    The SHT's latitude stage is a few thousand small, ragged matmuls against tables that never change. On Trainium the table is a stationary SBUF operand, the fields stream into fp32 PSUM, and the polar zeros vanish at trace time, which is why the kernel wins by 1.2–2.2× once enough fields share each table tile. The honest margin over carefully padded PyTorch is that, not orders of magnitude, and at one field the padding wins. What does need a library is everything PyTorch on the device can't express: the FFT, and a per-m loop that doesn't compile 770 programs. The forward beats ducc0 by 8.5–28×; the inverse loses to a two-line array reversal until trnfft#156 lands.
